# MASTER CHARACTER BIBLE — Islamic History Short-Form Films

**VERSION 1.0 — Read this file first.** This one file is designed to upload to an AI assistant as the main character database for a series. The complete production kit also contains detailed per-character MD files and reusable shot prompts.

## Rules for the director / AI video generator

1. Treat `CHR-xxx` as an immutable casting identifier throughout an episode and across episodes.
2. **Never invent a new face** when a validated individual portrait exists. When there is NO approved portrait, make a front-facing portrait first and ask for approval before generating multiple views/video.
3. This project's visual language is **REALISTIC HAND-DRAWN**: human skin pores, under-eye bags, scars, creases, age spots, subtle facial asymmetry, pencil hatching, sepia ink crosshatching, fine cloth texture, restrained colored washes, cinematic natural light. NOT cartoon, NOT anime, NOT plasticky photoreal CGI.
4. Expressions follow the story. Do not give everybody a random smile, a generic heroic look, or exaggerated grief. Default neutral/serious.
5. Only portray these as fictional background/supporting characters unless explicit verified historical evidence exists. Historical rulers, armies, garments and sites vary by century and region; verify before depicting specific real historical events.
6. Do not fabricate Qur'an quotes, hadith, or attributed historical lines. Verify independently. For sacred figures use respectful nonliteral approaches and do not label fictional faces as true likenesses.
7. Generate individual **3–6 second** 9:16 clips from reference images; stitch and narrate afterward.

## Core production setting

- Canvas: 9:16 vertical, target 1080 × 1920 or higher where available.
- Master look: warm parchment, charcoal ink, muted ochre, navy, burgundy, deep green, authentic natural skin tones.
- Mandatory cameras: eye-level front, left/right three-quarter, true left/right profile, back, low-angle, high-angle, extreme close-up, medium waist-up, over-shoulder, full body.
- Per-shot order: environment/time > character ID/reference > action > emotion > camera > movement > light > continuity > duration > negative constraints.

## Use one of these 12 precise camera codes

- `front_eye_level`: 0 degrees yaw; neutral eye level; symmetrical front-facing portrait, both eyes directed into lens, shoulders square; motion example: locked frame with imperceptibly slow push-in.
- `left_three_quarter`: face 45 degrees toward camera-left; three-quarter left view, both eyes visible but nose shows depth; motion example: slow cinematic arc from front to three-quarter left.
- `left_profile`: face 90 degrees to camera-left; true left-side profile, single visible eye, accurate nose and jaw silhouette; motion example: static side profile, minimal cloth movement.
- `back`: body yaw 180 degrees away; rear head and shoulder view, no visible face, keep costume, hair and silhouette; motion example: slow pullback revealing the environment.
- `right_profile`: face 90 degrees to camera-right; true right-side profile, single visible eye, mirror-consistent nose and jaw; motion example: static side profile with restrained breathing.
- `right_three_quarter`: face 45 degrees toward camera-right; three-quarter right view, same facial features and costume construction; motion example: slow cinematic arc from profile to three-quarter right.
- `low_angle`: camera approximately 25 degrees below eye level; heroic medium close-up from below, natural perspective without facial deformation; motion example: gentle tilt upward, no sudden perspective shift.
- `high_angle`: camera approximately 25 degrees above eye level; medium close-up from above, maintain recognizable facial anatomy; motion example: very slow descending camera drift.
- `extreme_closeup`: front, eye level, very tight framing; eyes, brow, cheeks and forehead fill frame; show nuanced eye moisture and real skin detail; motion example: subtle push-in toward the eyes.
- `medium_shot`: front, eye level, waist up; hands, costume, signature prop and expression visible; motion example: slow dolly inward while fabric moves naturally.
- `over_shoulder`: camera behind another person at eye level; main character in focus through foreground shoulder; keep eyes visible and geometry stable; motion example: subtle handheld drift, not shaky.
- `full_body`: front, eye level, entire figure and feet; complete anatomically plausible full figure, headwear, garment hem, footwear, props; motion example: slow tracking shot parallel to movement.

## Emotional expressions

- `neutral`: composed neutral face, not an automatic smile.
- `determined`: brows lowered slightly, steady direct gaze, jaw quietly tightened.
- `concerned`: subtle brow furrow, lips gently compressed, eyes alert.
- `grieving`: lowered or tearful eyes, fatigued facial muscles, grief without melodrama.
- `angry`: firm narrowed gaze, restrained tension in jaw, no exaggerated shouting unless written.
- `fearful`: widened eyes, constrained breath, shoulders slightly tensed.
- `reflective`: eyes fixed in thought, softened mouth, slight downward gaze.
- `hopeful`: softened brow, carefully controlled uplift in gaze, no gratuitous smile.
- `joyful`: subtle believable relief or smile only if event justifies it.
- `surprised`: raised brows and brief widened eyes, realistic not cartoonish.
